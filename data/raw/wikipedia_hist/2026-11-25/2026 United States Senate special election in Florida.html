The 2026 United States Senate special election in Florida was held on November 3, 2026, to elect a member of the United States Senate to represent the state of Florida. Republican appointee Ashley Moody defeated Democratic state representative Angie Nixon. She will complete the term won in 2022 by Marco Rubio, who resigned in 2025 to become secretary of state.

In the primary elections on August 18, Moody won the Republican nomination against minimal opposition. Alex Vindman, the former director of European Affairs for the National Security Council, was considered the Democratic frontrunner and held a significant fundraising advantage during the primary, but Nixon won the nomination with 56.1% of the vote in an upset.

Moody defeated Nixon by a closer-than-expected margin, winning by 2.9% compared with polling averages that had projected her to win by 6.1%.[1]

Background

[edit | edit source]

Marco Rubio, a U.S. senator from Florida since 2010, was announced as president-elect Donald Trump's nominee for secretary of state in November 2024.[2] He was confirmed by the Senate on January 20, 2025 in a 99-0 vote.[3] Ashley Moody, the attorney general of Florida, was named by Governor Ron DeSantis to fill Rubio's vacant Senate seat, and was sworn in the day after Rubio's confirmation.[4][5] Rubio's resignation triggered a special election for 2026, the next statewide election cycle in Florida. The victor of the special election will serve for the remainder of Rubio's term, until a regular election for a full term in 2028 is held.[6][7]

Democratic primary

[edit | edit source]

Campaign

[edit | edit source]

Angie Nixon, a progressive Democrat and a member of the Democratic Socialists of America, won the nomination over Alex Vindman, despite Vindman having amassed $16.3 million in campaign funding compared to Nixon's $975,000.[36][37][38][39] Nixon campaigned on universal healthcare, free childcare, and a national rent freeze.[40] Nixon had criticized Vindman for having only recently moved to Florida; Vindman ignored her and attacked the Republican candidate, Moody.[36] The result was considered a major upset.[36][37]

Analysis

[edit | edit source]

Moody only won this race by 2.9%, a significant reduction from Donald Trump's 13.1% margin in the presidential election and Rick Scott's 12.8% margin in the concurrent Senate election.[205] Nixon was able to flip Miami-Dade County, which Trump carried in 2024, back to the Democratic Party. She also improved on Kamala Harris's margin with Latino voters, winning the demographic by ten percentage points.[206][207] While Democrats turned out in higher numbers than Republicans, they were ultimately unable to overcome Republicans' 1.5 million voter registration advantage, which grew by 400,000 relative to 2024.[208][209][207] Additionally, outgoing governor Ron DeSantis credits Amendment 3, a property tax measure, with improving Republican turnout, helping Republicans win.[210] With Moody's victory, Florida was the only state that Trump won by less than 18 points in 2024 to have Republicans for both of its senators, according to Kyle Kondik of Sabato's Crystal Ball.[211]
